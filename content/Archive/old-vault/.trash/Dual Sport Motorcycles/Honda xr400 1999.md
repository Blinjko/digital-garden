## Getting it to start...
Oh boy there is a lot to say, here is the lot of it. Bike seems to start on unknown intervals, after many, many kicks. Too many kicks to be considered right even if it is big bore. Here is the data and thoughts I've collected thus far.

To start an engine we need, fuel + air + spark

### Flooding
I believe that the engine is flooding soon after the first 1-3 kicks. The reason I say this is because when I took the spark plug out the second time ( after I cleaned it ) and after many many kicks, it was covered in gas, even after sitting overnight. I also gave it a few kicks with the plug out to clear the engine since the decompression lever doesn't work due to the big bore. After putting the plug back in the first few kicks sounded very promising, but then they died off and the bike had no signs of life. So I took the plug out again and cleared the engine, and when I put it back in what do you know, a few promising kicks. So based on this observation and the fact that big bore motors need smaller jets than come stock, I think the bike is getting too much fuel on startup. The one time I did get it to start was after re-seating the carb in the intake manifold ( it wasn't fully in ) and clearing the engine. The bike only started with no choke and started on the 2-3rd kick. Odd... I was also running regular gas at the time too. All these things kinda lean me towards the idea that it is running rich. Haven't had here running since that last time ( maybe a pop once or twice ).
### Spark
Spark is good, could try different (new plug). Plug was super sooty when I pulled it to check, key sign she was running rich. I think a new plug might be a solution, especially given that it did start by the 2-3rd kick once with no tampering, and it hasn't since.

### Carb
The carb that was on the bike was the chinese replacement carb for the OEM. I have now taken this off and plan to put the OEM one back on ( I bought it with OEM off and chinese on). The seller claimed it leaked but from my testing it doesn't. The carb will be running the smallest jets I have, 60 pilot, and 142 main, I also believe I have the stock needle so I will put that in as well. The 60 pilot jet seems a bit large and I might have to get some smaller ones ( this I think has a high candidacy for being the problem. ). Currently just to get it started I don't think the needle or main jet matter so I'm not too worried about those yet. I'm hoping the OEM carb will perform better than the knock off one, but I am not sure yet.

### Valves
I read that the valves might cause really hard starting on these bikes, I checked all 4 and they are perfectly adjusted to spec. 0.10 mm intake, 0.12 mm exhaust.

### Airflow
I figured since I don't have any smaller jets on hand, I could make the mixture leaner by giving the engine more air, so I took out the snorkel. I am also playing with the AFR screw on the carb, starting as lean as can be and working through the choke settings 1 at a time after clearing the engine.


### Big bore
Big bore means the engine will suck more fuel through the jets so it is necessary to have smaller jets, usually smaller than the stock ones. The decompression lever doesn't work with the big bore, possibly due to valve piston interference, I know some kits have super tight tolerances. I also read that big bores can put more stress on the engine and cause things like the counter shaft, clutch / clutch basket, and the kick start to break. I am not sure if the owner did any of this work but well find out. I do not believe the bike is running different cams because the auto decompression still works, many say when running hot cams it doesn't work anymore. I am not sure what kit the person used and thus am unsure what compression ratio I am working with, which could cause me to need race fuel potentially.


### Trying to find the right AFR
My approach to trying to find the right AFR with the jets I have is to do the following.
1. Clear the engine by taking out the spark plug and kicking it a few times.
2. Set the AFR screw accordingly ( starting all the way in and slowly backing it out, probably 1/4 turn at a time.)
3. Set the choke accordingly, (1 choke setting per iteration, in the order of, off, mid, on)
4. Give it 5 kicks or so, and listen to how it sounds. Usually the first few sound promising then they all sound the same after 4-5 ( I think this is because it gets flooded ).
5. Repeat until I can at least get it started.
If this doesn't work I'll probably have to buy smaller jets and go through the process with them and hope I find a configuration that at least works.

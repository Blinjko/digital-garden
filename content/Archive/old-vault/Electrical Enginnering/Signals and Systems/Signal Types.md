## Continuous & Discrete Signals
Continuous and Discrete signals refer to time, which is usually the horizontal axis. A continuous signal exists for any point in time, whilst an discrete signal exists for distinct points in time only.

## Analogue & Digital Signals
Unlike continuous and discrete signals, Analogue and Digital signals refer to the vertical axis. An analogue signal is a signal that can take on any value for an amplitude, whilst a digital signal only has a certain amount of distinct amplitudes it can have, like how TTL logic signal only has 2 amplitudes, 0 and 5 volts.


## Periodic & Aperiodic Signal
A signal such that:
$$
x(t)=x(t+aT)
$$
for some value $T$, called the period, and some $a \in \mathbb{Z}$.
By this definition, periodic signals must start at negative infinity and continue forever until infinity. Thus they are also known as *everlasting signals*.

Aperiodic signals are signals that are not periodic.

## Everlasting Signal
A signal that begins at $-\infty$ and ends at $\infty$.


## Energy and Power Signals
See [[Signal Energy and Power]]
An energy signal is a signal with finite energy, i.e it cannot go on forever.
A power signal is a signal with finite nonzero power.


## Deterministic and Random Signals
A deterministic signal is a signal that has a known mathematical description.
A random signal is a signal that has a probabilistic description, and whose values cannot be predicted precisely.
